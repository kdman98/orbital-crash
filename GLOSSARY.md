# ORBITAL CRASH — Shared Glossary

A shared vocabulary so we can talk about the game precisely. **Bold** = the canonical
term to use in conversation. `code` = the identifier in `index.html`, for cross-reference.
If you want to rename anything, just say so and I'll update this file — it's the source of
truth for how we name things.

> **This file describes the game as it is now.** Nothing removed is documented here — for why
> something was changed or cut, read [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md), which is the dated ledger, or
> `git log`. Rows carry measurements only where the measurement is a live property.

## 1. The Star & its Field (you)

| Term | Meaning | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Star** | The wandering star you steer with the mouse. Your avatar. **Lowercase `world` never means you** — it is reserved for the coordinate space (*world space*, *design units* — see `WORLD SCALE` in the source) and for the game's setting (§8, "The silent world"). | `P` |
| **Polarity** | The Star's current charge — **Red** or **Cyan**. | `P.polarity` |
| **Field** | The circular magnetic field around the Star. **Like-charge** matter is caught into your orbital **Rings** (and leans toward you from outside the Field too — see **Core gravity**); **opposite-charge** matter gets no magnetic pull at all, ever — it only drifts in under its own chase, and annihilates against you. | `P.fieldR`, `P.fieldK` |
| **Ring hysteresis** | Once a body is in your ring it **stays yours for 0.8s** after a move outruns it, and the spring keeps reeling it back the whole time. Beyond **2.4× the Field** it is genuinely gone rather than lagging. **Direction reversal is what sheds a ring, not sustained speed** — the follow is `(pointer − P) × 0.185` uncapped, so a corner-to-corner flick peaks at **135 px/frame**. Retention: flick every 0.75s → **93%**, every 0.42s and 0.25s → **100%**, sustained strafing at 45 and 80 px/frame → **100%**. A 300px displacement (a dodge) keeps 10/10; a 600px one drops to 4. | `RING_GRACE`, `RING_GRACE_R`, `e.ringGrace` |
| **Core gravity** | The Star's pull on its **own colour**, and only outside the Field: full at the rim, fading to zero at **1.8×** the Field. Like-charge closing under that pull also gets **ring-grade speed headroom (2.9)**, so a dodging player does not outrun their own ammunition — measured avg ring **3.3 dodging vs 2.6 still**. Approach from 300 / 260 / 220px takes **1.23 / 0.73 / 0.35s** against **1.47 / 0.97 / 0.48s** for the hostile colour, which is untouched: the Star never sucks in another charge. Deliberately small next to a Drifter's own 0.22 seek — your colour should lean toward you, not be vacuumed. Approach settles cleanly (272→227→154→111→**114px**, no overshoot, no re-exits). | `LIKE_GRAV`, `LIKE_GRAV_R`, `closing` |
| **Rings** | Like-charge matter held in orbit around the Star. Simultaneously **armour** (it annihilates opposite-charge matter before it reaches you — it does *not* stop Anomaly missiles, which pass through everything), **fuel**, and — via the grind (§7) — a **weapon on contact**, so a loaded ring is dangerous to an Anomaly before you ever spend it. Against the swarm a charged Reverse spends the ring to *widen the Fling* — and then flings the ring itself. Against an **Anomaly** the same act **volleys** it (§7) — the one place rings become projectiles, because there they finally have something to be aimed at. | `e.ring`, `P.ringMul` |
| **Ring spin** | The ring orbits at **4.8 px/frame — one revolution every 2.49s** at `fieldR·0.6` (114px). Steady state is `spin/(1-0.80)`, where `0.80` is the extra velocity bleed rings carry so they hold their radius. **`reduceMotion` takes another 30% off** → 3.4 px/frame, 3.44s per revolution. Under **Overdrive/Eddy** it goes wide *and* fast, where a **Brute clamps** against its own ceiling while a Drifter does not — mass shows in the ring, but only once the ring out-paces the slowest bodies in it. | `spin` in the ring block, `DOTSPD` |
| **Integrity** | Your health. Regenerates `2.6/s`, but only after **3.8s without a hit**, and nothing else heals. **Disengaging is the only heal.** | `P.hp`, `P.maxHp`, `P.hurtT` |

**The central rule, stated once:** same-colour matter is **harmless** and passes through your core; opposite-colour matter **damages you** on contact. So a wall of one colour is defused by matching it — that is why **Patterns** (§6) alternate colour on *every body*.

## 2. Player verbs (things you do)

| Term | Meaning | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Reverse (poles)** | Switch Polarity — a **pole reversal** (click / space). Short cooldown (0.28s) — deliberate, not spammable. | `flip()` |
| **Shockwave** | The outward push every reversal emits (repels matter, **pops a Neutral outright** — one reversal, not two). 0.5s cooldown — a tool, not a constant shield. Its radius and force both scale by `1 + hold-charge × 0.5`, so a tap reaches 104px and a fully-loaded reversal reaches 156px. **It does not touch an Anomaly at all** — see §7. The Neutral pop is a **kill, not damage** — see §5. | `P.pulseCd`, `P.conduitAmp` |
| **Hold-charge** | Charge that accrues while you *hold* a Polarity. It builds on **time since your last flip**, not on holding a button. Past ~0.75s the Rings are **loaded**. | `P.holdT`, `P.holdMax` |
| **Hungry flip** | A reversal made while **loaded**. It does two things at once: **flings** the matter that just turned hostile away from you, and **volleys** your Rings at any Anomaly on the field. See below. This, not body contact, is the game's real weapon. | in `flip()`, `RING_FIRE_HC` |
| **Annihilation** | The core kill event: two opposite-charge things touch → both destroyed. | `queueKill`, `processKills` |
| **Two-wave release** | Rule 2 alternates every body in a shape, so if a formation's flight lapsed on ONE frame every body would be standing next to its own annihilator. The release is therefore staggered **by polarity** — one colour lets go `NOOSE_WAVE` (0.8s) after the other, so adjacent bodies are never loose in the field together. Used by the **Noose** and the **Wall**. Measured: **0 pair-kills across 8 walls / 199 bodies**, both orientations. | `NOOSE_WAVE`, `formNoose`, `formWall` |
| **Silhouette law** | Every species owns a **distinct outline or internal marking**, and **colour is never one of them** — hue is reserved for polarity. Drifter (plain disc + core) is the deliberate **null**: legibility is differential, so the unmarked slot is spent on the most common body rather than wasted. Dart = backward wake, Mini = no core (solid pellet), Orbiter = annulus + clockwise pip, Brute = hexagon, Splitter = twin lobes, Neutral = red/cyan halves on a turning seam, Bomber = 7-point star + fuse, Charger = arrowhead (**solid** armed, **hollow** spent). Marks are **negative space, not stacked fills** — the enemy pass runs under `lighter`, where layered fills saturate to white and erase the polarity contrast the whole colour law depends on. Ceiling: nine species is the edge of what a shape vocabulary carries at 40+ simultaneous bodies; a tenth should reuse a silhouette and differ by *behaviour*. | `drawEnemies` |
| ⚠️ **The danger edge** | Anything drawn **outside** a body reads as "the edge where I die", and the real envelope is **`e.r + P.r` = `e.r + 15`** (`P.r` set once, never reassigned). **The rule: a ring outside a hull must sit at exactly `e.r + P.r`, or not exist.** The defect is the false *value*, not the geometry. Live cases that obey it: the Charger's **reticle** (collapses to `e.r + P.r` = 28) and the **Anomaly's integrity ring** (`b.r + P.r` = 52 against a 37px hull). The **universal halo** is exempt by decision — it is a cached radial-gradient sprite per polarity with a luminance cliff of **9** at its edge, so there is no boundary left to aim at, and sizing it to `e.r+15` would put a 22px cloud on a 7px Mini. ✅ **The last false radius, the Gilded ring, was fixed 2026-08-03** — `1.8r` → `e.r + P.r`. Every ring drawn outside a hull now obeys the rule. | `glowSprite`, `drawEnemies` |
| **…and the star's own edge is `P.r`** | The rule cuts both ways, and the star was the only body breaking it in the *dangerous* direction. Its crisp white rim sat at `P.r*0.86` = **12.9** against a real radius of 15, so you died 2.1px before your own hull touched anything. The rim is now inset by half its stroke width so its **outer edge lands on exactly `P.r`**, and the body fills to `P.r` underneath it — move one without the other and the rim detaches into a floating ring. **What this buys is one rule with no exceptions: two hulls meeting on screen IS the frame of contact.** star 15 + Drifter 11 = 26 = `e.r + P.r`, and the same holds for all nine species and the Anomaly. Measured on the rendered frame: full-bright rim out to 14.44, then the partially-covered boundary pixel at **15.00**. ✅ **And it is now the star's only edge.** Two flat-alpha atmosphere discs used to sit at 1.45r and 2.4r; flat alpha means a *hard* boundary however soft the colour looks, and they measured luminance cliffs of **51** at 22px and **73** at 36px against the **9** a gradient measures. Both were outside the hull, so they were two more rings claiming to be an edge on the one body whose edge has to be unambiguous. **Deleted rather than converted** — `drawField` already lays a real radial gradient under the star (0.16 → 0 over `P.fieldR`), so the soft light was already there and it fades to nothing. Measured after: biggest cliff outside the hull **4**, and the ray decays 51 → 43 monotonically from 16px to 40px. | `drawPlayer` |
| **Same-charge shove** | Like charges cannot annihilate, so same-colour bodies **take up space** instead: an overlap is resolved **positionally** at `SAME_PUSH` (half per frame), split by **mass**, so a Brute shoulders a Drifter aside rather than being nudged equally. **Positions only, never velocity** — the field owns velocity, and impulse here would pump energy into the ring orbit. **Formation bodies are exempt**, because `holdBody`/`holdOrbit` write x/y every frame: a shove would be overwritten or would corrupt measured geometry. Measured: 14 stacked bodies at **0.9px** mean nearest-neighbour spread to **21.8px** (body diameter 22) in 1.5s; the player's ring held **114.2 → 114.3px** with spread 0.4 against a 114 design radius, because the shove between ring bodies is tangential. Consequence: less incidental mutual annihilation, so the live field is denser — median bodies **42**. | `SAME_PUSH`, `stepAnnihilation` |

### The Fling (the hungry flip)

A quick **tap** just reverses poles. A reversal made past `RING_FIRE_HC` **flings**: every body that is opposite-charge *after the flip resolves* and inside the burst is **thrown outward, alive**. Nothing dies.

Why that framing matters: a tap already turns the swarm harmless, because matching its colour is what safety means here. What makes reversing dangerous under pressure is that **your own Rings and your own gathered matter turn hostile the instant you flip** — and they are in close orbit. The Fling throws exactly that off you.

| Term | Meaning | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Fling radius** | `80 + charge·70 + min(rings,10)·6` → **80px to 210px**. Charge sets the floor; **matter you actually gathered sets the ceiling**, so Rings are armour on the way in and reach on the way out. A full burst reaches just **past** your 190px Field, so the circle you can see is the promise. | `PURGE_R0`, `PURGE_RCHG`, `PURGE_RPER` |
| **Fling impulse** | `10.8 → 18.0 px/frame`, scaled by proximity — hardest point-blank, and never below **65%** out at the rim. Launch speed at full charge is **15.8 px/frame**, time to peak **0.96s**. For **0.8s** the body skips the speed clamp, runs on 0.985 friction and has **seek at zero** — dead straight, or the seek (0.22/frame → ~12px/frame inward) would cancel the impulse and drag it back onto the core. | `FLING_V0`, `FLING_VCHG`, `FLING_HOLD` |
| **What a flip actually clears** | Measured with 30 bodies packed around the core, by hold-charge. **Minimum hungry (0.55):** peak **441px**. **0.75:** **508px**. **Full:** **689px**. The Field is swept clean in **0.17–0.27s** in every case, and *nothing is lost* — every body flies out, decelerates and comes home. Hold longer, breathe longer. | `FLING_HOLD` |
| **What it costs you** | Against an Anomaly a hungry flip spends your **Rings** — the ammunition you held a pole to gather — so flinging for safety and volleying for damage draw on the same pot. It does not cost you cover: nothing is cover, since missiles pass through all matter (§7). | — |
| **Never flung** | **Neutrals** (uncharged — the Shockwave's job), and **Rings while an Anomaly is alive** (they are the Volley's business instead). Everything else is ordinary flingable matter, Bombers included. | in `flip()` |
| **Spent rings** | With an Anomaly alive they are **volleyed** at it (§7). With none, they take **the full fling** — they are not ammunition any more, they are 20-odd bodies that just turned hostile in close orbit. | in `flip()` |

### Collapse

| Term | Meaning | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Collapse** | Spend a full **Capacitor** (Shift / R-click). A **0.45s inhale** under 0.2x slow-motion, then an arena-sweeping **blast wave: 2 damage, one hit per wave** — trash dies, Brutes crawl out burned and flung. Chips the Anomaly **15%, flat**. | `collapse()`, `detonateCollapse()`, `cwave` |
| **…against an Anomaly** | **15% and nothing more, whatever the field looks like.** The wave itself never touches the boss, and neither do the chain-blasts its kills set off. Density must not be able to kill a boss. | `processKills()`, `unstable` |
| **Tally** | A Collapse of **8+ kills** pays a bonus of **N²×4**. 40+ in one is a feat. | `collapseKills` |

> ⚠️ **Open design question: Collapse has the least distinct identity of any earned action.** It is one of two "everything near me dies" effects (with Nova), it costs the most to reach, and its inhale is a dead pause. The player's verdict after three recorded runs: *"i don't feel collapse is fun enough or something interesting to win hard."*
>
> **And there is no measurement to argue with it, because the scripted pilot has never once detonated a Collapse.** `.oracle.js` calls `g.collapse()` every 311 frames, but `collapse()` refuses unless `P.charge>=1 && inhaleT<=0 && unstable<=0`, and the oracle drives `step()` directly so the real-time `inhaleT` countdown in `frameBody` never runs. `inhaleT` is pinned at 0.45 after the first successful call and every later one is refused; the blast never fires. Human usage across 322 recorded seconds: **two presses**. Every Collapse number in this file rests on human play alone — its bot-derived numbers are not weak evidence, they are no evidence.

## 3. Meters & resources

| Term | Meaning | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Capacitor** | The meter that fills toward a Collapse. Fed by kills, Motes, streak milestones and Epoch clears. Chimes when full. | `P.charge`, `chgbar` |
| **Streak** | A **no-hit** combo — resets only on real damage. Named tiers at 25/50/100/200: **AWAKENING / MAGNETIC / UNSTOPPABLE / TRANSCENDENT**, each paying a Capacitor chunk. It pays **no score at all**: Streak feeds the Capacitor, Motes feed the score, and that split is the whole reason both exist. On loss a 25+ streak **bursts** into Capacitor instead of vanishing — and the burst is the *only* thing a broken streak prints, because breaking one is a hit and a hit says one thing (§10). | `combo`, `streakTier`, `breakStreak` |
| **Mote** | Annihilation loot carrying the popped body's colour. A kill sheds **1–2**. Same-polarity Motes hoover to the Star; opposite ones lie inert until a reversal **vacuums** them. Each is worth `MOTE_SCORE` where it is picked up, plus a sliver of Capacitor — so sweeping your own debris pays about a third again on top of the kill that made it. Nothing is banked and nothing is lost on a hit. | `motes`, `MOTE_SCORE` |
| **Score** | **Plain addition — there is no multiplier.** Kill **20**, Mote **5**, Graze **10**; Gilded Bounty `250×epoch`, Anomaly purge `200×epoch`, Collapse tally `N²×4`. Every annihilation pays the same wherever it happens: where you stand is already priced by the things that decide the fight — closing the range is how a Volley connects, and it is what walks you into an Anomaly's point-blank fire. | `KILL_SCORE`, `MOTE_SCORE`, `GRAZE_SCORE`, `onKill` |
| ⚠️ **…and the Collapse tally is out of scale with the rest — OPEN** | `N²×4` is quadratic against a flat income table, so a 20-kill Collapse pays **1,600** on top of the **400** the kills themselves pay — a **+400%** bonus. This is **not** new: at the multiplier's *measured* median of ×1.9 it was already **+421%**, so removing the multiplier barely moved it. It shows up as run-to-run score variance of **1,070 to 7,706** across 12 identical-pilot runs, decided almost entirely by whether one fat Collapse landed. Left alone deliberately — whether hoarding *should* dominate scoring is a design call, not a consequence of dropping the multiplier. `N²×1` is the one-line change if it should not. | `collapseKills` |
| **Graze** | A dangerous body that skims the Star and leaves without touching — **10** and a sfx only. Pays **no** Capacitor: charge income should be chosen, not lucked into. | `e.grz`, `GRAZE_SCORE` |
| **Gilded Bounty** | Every 12–18s one body arrives gold-ringed; pop it within 6s for a jackpot. Suppressed in Boss Rush. **It is only ever a Drifter or a Dart** (`Math.random()<0.5?'drift':'fast'`) — never a big body, never a Bomber. Its mark is a **dashed gold ring drawn at exactly `e.r + P.r`** (26 on a Drifter) — the only mark in the game drawn outside a hull, so it obeys §2 and is simultaneously the bounty cue *and* the true contact edge. Affordable only because a Gilded body is rare and unique; forty of these would be a HUD rather than a cue. | `e.gild`, `gildTimer` |
| **Pattern Lab** | A third run mode beside Boss Rush, from the menu. A live ambient field with **no Anomaly and no Epoch phases**, and **keys 1-5 fire the five shapes on demand** — Wall · Noose · Pulse · Sorter · Comet. Auto-formations are suppressed in it, so nothing arrives unless you asked for it. It exists because Boss Rush cannot serve this: formations are gated on `wavePhase!=='boss'`, which is most of what Boss Rush is. Like Boss Rush, it cannot set the best score. | `labMode`, `beginLabRun`, `labFire`, `LAB_SHAPES` |
| **Achievement** | An in-run feat, recorded in the Codex. **Flavour only** — they unlock nothing (there is no meta economy). **Five** exist; **one** is **secret**, shown as "???" until discovered. Nothing in code counts the rows — the Codex maps over `ACHV` — so adding or retiring one is a single line. | `ACHV`, `store.achv` |

## 4. Powerups (the only progression)

Any annihilation can shed a glowing **orb**; steer into it to collect. The effect is **instant and temporary** — grabbing the same type again refreshes its timer. A purged Anomaly always drops one **cache**, and a safety orb floats in if the field has been empty a while. Never duplicates something already active or already waiting.

| Term | Effect | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Orb** | The floating pickup itself. Max 2 on the field; ~0.8% per kill; safety drop every 22–34s when the field is bare. | `orbs`, `dropOrb`, `ORB_DROP_CHANCE` |
| **🛡 Aegis** | 6s shield, 3 hits. The only shield source. | `FX.aegis`, `P.shield` |
| **⚡ Overdrive** | 6s — faster star, wide whirling rings. | `FX.overdrive`, `P.eddy` |
| **✺ Nova** | Instant free Collapse wave (no charge cost). Rarer roll. | `fireNova()` |

**Roll rates**, measured over 6,000 rolls against the three-wide roster: **Aegis 45.1%, Overdrive 43.9%, Nova 11.0%** — Nova carries a `noNova` down-weight, the other two split the rest. Aegis is the most common pickup in the game.

## 5. Matter (the regular enemies)

**Matter** (a **body**) = any regular (non-boss) enemy. Each carries a charge; opposite-charge bodies are your threat *and* your ammo. To **pop** a body = destroy it.

| Term | Meaning | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Drifter** | Baseline body, steady approach. | `drift` |
| **Dart** | Small, very fast, low damage. | `fast` |
| **Brute** | Big, slow, tanky (3 hp), heavy hit — **22, the hardest contact hit in the sky**. The only body that walks out of a **Collapse** (2 dmg) at 1 hp, burned, and the only body that survives a **Bomber blast**. Nothing erases it outright: it has to be annihilated by colour like anything else, which makes it the most durable body in the game. | `heavy` |
| **Splitter** | Drawn as a **binary** — two overlapping lobes with two nuclei, the only divided silhouette in the sky. Bursts into exactly **2 Minis** when destroyed (unless killed during a Collapse or by a Bomber blast, neither of which runs `onKill`, so neither leaves fragments). | `split` |
| **Mini** | Tiny fast fragment spawned by a Splitter. | `mini` |
| **Orbiter** | Curves *around* the Star instead of beelining. (Distinct from the **Sentinel** Anomaly.) | `orbiter` |
| **Bomber** | An **ordinary dot in every stat** — `dmg` **10** (Drifter parity), **1 hp**, no exemptions anywhere — that **detonates when it dies**. The spiked hull and blinking fuse are a warning, not just species identity. | `bomber`, `bomberBlast` |
| **…and what the detonation does and does not do** | **120px, colour-blind, 2 damage** — Collapse parity, so trash dies and a Brute (3hp) crawls out burned. Colour-blind is load-bearing: your rings are *your own colour*, so a blast that respected polarity could not remove ring shield at all, which is the entire point. Sized just past the ring orbit radius (`P.fieldR*0.6` = **114**), so a Bomber that reaches your core clears the hoard and one popped out on the ring takes a bite of it. **Three exclusions:** it **does not touch you** (contact stays 10; the blast adds nothing at the core); it **does not touch the Anomaly** (a boss walking through a crowded field must not be killed by density — §7); and it **pays nothing** — `dead` alone, never `queueKill`, so no score, combo, Mote or Capacitor. That last one is the point rather than a limitation: the brief is to *punish* hoarding, and a paying blast would make hoard-then-pop a score fountain rewarding the behaviour it exists to remove. It also **does not chain** — a Bomber killed by a blast dies via `dead`, so `onKill` never runs and it never detonates. **Measured:** a 16-body ring at radius 114 → **0** survivors. Full species sweep, one of each of the nine seated 70px out — **8 of 9 destroyed; the Brute is the only survivor, at 1 of 3 hp**; the Neutral dies (2 hp, exactly the blast); a control Drifter at 300px lives; the Splitter dies **without splitting**; score paid **0** for the eight and Motes **0**. A second Bomber inside the blast dies without detonating. In an ordinary field it clears a median of **2** bodies, up to **9** in a dense one — the bite scales with local density, which is why it lands hardest on a packed ring. | `BOMB_R`, `BOMB_DMG`, `sfx.bomb` |
| **Charger** | Ignores your Field — **the only body your magnetism does not own**, so it is drawn as an **arrowhead** pointed along its heading rather than a soft disc. Every other body is something you gather or annihilate; this one travels on a line of its own and will never join your ring. A **committed dash** driven into an Anomaly is worth **8** (see the two-channel rule), and the connect announces itself: hitstop, a screen kick, twin rings at the impact point and a gold **◆ BAITED −8** at purge scale, with its own voice (`sfx.baited`). Read the setup honestly — the lane locks along the charger→**you** ray, so the only way it crosses the Anomaly is to put the Anomaly on that line, which means **standing in the kill lane and leaving late**. Winds up **0.9s** → dashes 250px. It **locks its end point the instant the wind-up starts** and draws that lane in full, to a fixed reticle collapsing on the end point — so the line is a promise, and stepping off it is the answer; it re-derives its heading from where it actually is (it drifts while winding) and ends on **distance covered**, not on a clock. A dashing Charger **plows through opposite-charge matter** — bait its dash across the swarm. It gets **one lunge at a time**: after the dash it is `spent` for **`CHG_COOL`=5s**, hull hollow and inert, the filling wedge counting its cooldown down. **Kill it in that window and it is gone; leave it and it re-arms** — and if you *match* its colour it rides your ring as harmless armour, then re-arms **inside your guard** (`e.ring=0` on the re-arm, so it leaves the ring at ring radius, ~117px). Stay opposite and your own ring annihilates it for free. Verified: standing still is hit **8/8**; stepping perpendicular off the lane, **0/8** (126px clear). | `charger`, `CHG_WIND`, `CHG_REACH`, `CHG_COOL` |
| **Neutral** | The body that **wears both poles** — half red, half cyan, split down a turning seam. It is the one body the colour law does not reach: the Field ignores it, the **Fling ignores it** (it is uncharged, not "the other colour"), and no polarity you can hold makes it safe or makes it ammunition — so it carries both charges instead of a colour of its own. **One Shockwave pops it — a kill, whatever its HP,** rather than damage that happens to equal it. Damage cannot express that rule safely: the pulse fires only every 0.5s however fast you flip, and a damaged Neutral is drawn identically to a fresh one, so *any* multi-hit kill makes the first hit invisible and the body reads as immune. It is **not** cover — nothing is; missiles pass through every body (§7). | `neutral` |

## 6. Patterns (irregular waves)

Ambient spawning is edge-random, so it reads as weather. Every so often the field instead does something with obvious **intent** — you get a beat of "…what?" before you read the shape. Every pattern flies in from off-screen, which telegraphs it without a word of text.

**Three rules make a pattern a pattern**, and a shape that breaks any one of them is decorative:

1. **Spacing under 52px.** Contact happens at core `r15` + body `r11` = **26px** centre-to-centre, so any two bodies spaced wider than 52 have a walkable midpoint between them. `FORM_STEP=44` keeps every line genuinely solid.
2. **Alternate every body.** Same-colour matter passes through the core untouched (§1), so *any* run of two same-colour bodies is a free door costing one keypress. Per-body alternation means you always straddle two opposites — **no polarity is ever safe**.
3. **Never end on a timer alone.** A shape that just crosses and dissolves is beaten by standing still. The Wall's gap *shuts* and it *returns*; the Noose *locks shut* and then *bites*.

| Term | Meaning | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Pattern** | A hand-placed wave. First lands ~42s in, then every 30–46s. **Paused during a boss**, skipped above 200 bodies. | `spawnFormation()`, `formT`, `FORMS` |
| **Linear flight** | While `hold` runs, a body flies its assigned vector and ignores seek *and* the polarity field — otherwise every shape converges on the core and dissolves into ordinary drizzle. `turnT` reverses it; `lvx/lvy` + `latT` drive the closing door. | `e.hold`, `e.fvx/fvy`, `e.turnT`, `e.latT` |
| **Polar flight** | The Noose's and Pulse's mode: the body holds an *angle and radius* about a centre and both are stepped each frame. Needed because a constant-velocity tangent travels a straight line and would miss the centre by `R·sin(atan(va/vr))` — the ring would never actually shut. `o.rmin` floors the radius so a shrinking ring stops before it reaches its own self-contact spacing. | `holdOrbit()`, `e.orb`, `o.rmin` |
| **The Wall** | A solid line spans the arena and marches across. **One gap**, and the gap *shuts* as it advances (the flanking bodies slide inward), so it is widest the moment it arrives. At the far edge the whole line **turns around** and comes back once. | `formWall` |

> **A ring near your core always eats itself — that is the field, not the shape.** Control: sixteen *ordinary* bodies simply placed on a 106px ring around a still player, never in a formation, annihilate **16 of 16**. The two colours converge on you at very different speeds and cross. So no formation that obeys rule 2 can end cleanly near the core; it can only be less bad than raw matter in the same place. The Noose loses **4 of 15.5**. Don't chase it to zero — the remaining path runs through rule 2 or the colour law itself.

## 7. The Anomaly (boss)

> ### ⚠️ THE TWO-CHANNEL RULE — do not add a third
>
> **A body erodes the Anomaly if and only if it is (a) a VOLLEY you fired at it, (b) a RING you gathered and carried into it, or (c) the one sanctioned exception, a CHARGER in its committed dash that you stood so as to aim.** Everything else that touches it pays **zero** and bounces off. Not drifting matter, not flung matter, not heavy matter. Three branches, and the expression in the boss-contact block should stay three branches.
>
> **The reason is that the Anomaly moves.** The Hunt commits to the core, so it walks the length of the arena through a field full of matter — *any* rule shaped like "opposite-charge contact hurts it" means **the boss damages itself by travelling** and the player need do nothing. The damage list therefore cannot be about what touches it; it has to be about what **you aimed** or **what you carried**. A volley you fired and a ring you gathered and steered are both things you did; drifting into it, being flung past it, and being heavy are things that merely happen.
>
> **Contact is not achievement.** If a future channel is proposed, the test is: *could the Anomaly earn this by moving?* If yes, it does not belong. This rule has had to be re-tightened four separate times; it is at the top of the section rather than buried in a row for that reason.
>
> **Why the Charger passes that test.** Its dash is a lane it locks and draws, so putting the Anomaly on that lane is something **you positioned for**. Gated on `cst==='dash'`, so one merely drifting into the boss pays nothing like anything else. The accident rate is a property of the **geometry** rather than the price: **0 accidental hits across 8 idle 45s fights** — the boss cannot walk into it — while a deliberate line-up lands the full hit and consumes the Charger, and the same setup off the line lands **0**.
>
> ⚠️ **`CHARGE_DMG` is stated in five places.** `index.html` is the source of truth; the Charger row (§5), the Ways-in row (§7), the bestiary card and **the in-game Codex** all restate it. **If you reprice it, grep the literal across `index.html`, `GLOSSARY.md` and `bestiary.html` before you commit** — the Codex matters most, because it is the only copy a player ever sees. It is pinned at **4 × `VOLLEY_DMG`**; keep it that.

| < 80s | drift 42 · fast 26 · heavy 16 · orbiter 10 · charger 6 |
| < 125s | drift 32 · fast 22 · heavy 15 · orbiter 12 · charger 10 · bomber 9 |
| ≥ 125s | drift `max(8,30−3a)` · fast 20 · heavy `12+a` · orbiter 12 · charger `8+a` · bomber `7+a` · split `4+a` · neutral `3+floor(a/2)` |

During a boss fight the colour leans mildly toward the **opposite of the Anomaly's** so there is ammunition, but stays varied — the reliable kill is the flip, not the lean.

### The silent world

There is **no centre banner**. Storms, Epochs, the Anomaly's arrival, Collapse and streak tiers are announced by **matter, colour, ring, shake and sound** — never by text across the middle of the screen, which pulls the eyes off the field at exactly the wrong moment. Two text channels exist, both out of the play area:

- **Pickup pill** (left, by the chips) — a powerup's *effect* is the one thing you genuinely can't read off the screen.
- **Achievement toast** (top centre, gold).

A tally *of the thing you just watched happen* is not a third channel — it breaks the rule for the same reason a banner does. The reversal announces itself with a ring drawn at the burst's true radius, the bodies visibly launching, a screen kick and a sound; a number adds nothing you can act on.

## 11. Identifiers that look dead and are not

Every one of these sits at an identity value or in an empty state, so a sweep for dead code will find them. They are live and must stay.

|---|---|
| `ruptureBlast()` | The Aegis block calls it on every shield break. |

Measured, it bought **0.64 of one frame** — the gap closes at a median 3.9 px/frame at the moment of contact, so any grace under ~4px is smaller than a single frame of approach and cannot be perceived.
